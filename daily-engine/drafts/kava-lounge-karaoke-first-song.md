# Karaoke at a kava lounge: a first‑song plan

Going to karaoke at a kava lounge? Short answer: decide before you arrive whether you want to sing or simply soak in the room, pick an easy, familiar song as your first mic choice, and use the lounge’s menu and staff to keep the evening comfortable. Below is a small, practical plan so you won’t spend the first five minutes of the night scrolling for a song.

## Confirm the night before (fact vs. planning)

Fact: Tribal lists karaoke as a rotating event and says the next start time is posted on their Instagram. Check Tribal’s events page or their Instagram to confirm the night and start time before you head over (don’t rely on memory from last month).

- Why this matters: rotating nights can change, and the events page explicitly asks guests to confirm the next rotating night before making plans.

Useful step: screenshot the Instagram post or save the events page link so you’re not hunting for it at the door.

## Decide now: sing or listen?

Pick one clear intention before you arrive. It fixes everything else.

- If you plan to sing: pick a short, comfortable first song and arrive a little early so you can scope the mic queue and ask staff how signups work that night. Bringing a phone note with your chosen song title and artist makes the mic handoff smoother.
- If you plan to listen: choose a comfortable spot near the action but not on the stage, and think about one or two conversation starters (song compliments are obvious winners). You’ll be ready if a friend drags you up later.

This is a plan, not a rule: you can always change your mind at the lounge.

## Choosing a forgiving first song

Goal: something short, clearly melodic, and within a comfortable vocal range. Avoid long narratives, very wide ranges, or songs that rely on studio auto-tune flourishes you can’t reproduce live.

Practical criteria:

- Familiarity: pick a song you can hum from start to finish without reading lyrics. Familiarity saves you from the panic of searching mid‑line.
- Tempo: medium tempo is easier than very slow ballads (which expose pitch) or very fast rap (which demands precise rhythm and breath control).
- Range: choose a song that sits in the middle of your range; if you don’t know your range, pick songs by singers with similar voices to yours.
- Length: 3–3:30 minutes is a safe length for a first go — enough to feel like a full performance without draining you.

Examples of safe approaches:

- Pop singalongs with clear choruses
- Classic rock or upbeat R&B hits with repeatable refrains
- Simple duets or call-and-response numbers if you have a friend to share the mic

Avoid promising a guaranteed crowd reaction; pick what keeps you comfortable.

## Before you order: use the menu to match the vibe

If you want a non-botanical drink or something mild before your mic debut, Tribal’s menu lists fruit drinks and crafted kava options; the menu also explains shell and tea formats and flavor choices. Ask the kavatender for a flavor-first recommendation or a non-kava fruit drink if you’re unsure.

Useful tip: order something you can sip steadily — it keeps your throat from drying without turning into a performance prop.

Want to check options ahead of time? Scan Tribal’s menu and the visit page so you arrive ready to order and know where to sit.

## At the lounge: rhythm, not rules

- Ask staff how signups work that night. The events page reminds guests to confirm rotating nights and start times before heading over, so the in-room routine can vary.
- Keep your first song short and smile. A relaxed delivery often reads better than a technically perfect but tense performance.
- If you chicken out, no harm done. Clap, stay, and pick a later, easier song.

## Two quick follow-ups

- Check the events page for the official note that karaoke nights are rotating and that the next start time will be on Instagram. This is the authoritative place to confirm the date.
- Glance at the menu before you go so you can make a quick choice at the counter and focus on your mic moment.

One next step: confirm the next karaoke night on Tribal’s events page and scan the menu so you arrive ready to sing or listen.
