# Creative nights at a kava lounge: plan your project first

Start with one question: do you want to bring and work on your own project, or do you want a guided session where materials and a plan are provided? At Tribal the difference is simple—Art Club invites you to bring your own project or join a guided painting, while Sip & Paint is presented as a daytime paint session with materials included and a bundled drink offer—but rotating nights mean you should confirm the next session’s details with the team before you go (see the events page).

## What the two formats really mean

- Art Club (bring-your-own-project): Tribal’s events listing describes Art Club as a night where you can bring your own project or join a guided painting session. The default expectation is that you can work on personal art, crafts, or practice, with the option to take part in a guided activity if one is scheduled. Bring your own supplies and a project bag suited to the work you plan to do.

- Sip & Paint (guided, materials-included): The events listing presents Sip & Paint as a daytime paint session with materials included and a drink bundled into the event offer. If you sign up for Sip & Paint, plan to follow a project plan led by an instructor and expect materials to be provided—again, confirm the current offer before you arrive.

(These distinctions come from Tribal’s events listing; rotating nights require confirmation before you go.)

## How to decide: personal project vs. guided session

Choose Art Club when:
- You’re bringing a work-in-progress or a medium that needs your own tools (complex mixed media, specialty brushes, etc.).
- You want flexible time to start, stop, and tweak without following a preset lesson.

Choose Sip & Paint when:
- You want a relaxed, taught experience with materials laid out and a single-session project.
- You prefer a structured start-to-finish plan and like the convenience of supplied materials and a bundled drink option.

If you’re unsure, message or call Tribal ahead of time. The events page notes rotating art nights and directs guests to check event details or Instagram for the next confirmed date—so confirm the session type, registration, and what’s actually included before you go.

## What to bring to Art Club (clear, practical checklist)

- Compact project bag: sketchbook, paints, palette, pens, brushes, and a small container for water or solvents if your medium needs it.
- Surface protection: a small plastic mat, cardboard, or towel to guard the table against spills.
- Portable drying solution: a cardboard panel, folder, or collapsible drying rack for wet canvases—ask staff about leaving work to dry rather than assuming storage.
- Cleanup kit: paper towels, a small trash bag, and any solvent-safe wipes you use.
- Snacks or water if you prefer—Tribal’s menu lists drinks and options you can order while you create.

Label anything you might leave on a table and carry a portfolio or folder for finished pieces. If your medium is unusually messy or requires special handling, confirm with the team before bringing it in.

## What to expect if you sign up for Sip & Paint

Sip & Paint is described as a session where materials are included and a drink may be bundled with registration. That generally means canvases, basic paints, brushes, and a guided plan are provided. But a past flyer or Instagram post does not guarantee the next session’s exact date, price, supplies, or bundled items—confirm registration details, cancellation policy, and what’s included with Tribal before you arrive.
