# Mint, citrus, or sweet heat: choosing a crafted kratom flavor

Start with the basic facts: the menu lists three crafted kratom drinks with short, specific flavor notes — Mango Chili Lime is described as “mango, lime, and a sweet-heat finish,” Passionfruit Mint as “passionfruit, mint, and citrus,” and Blackberry Dragonfruit Fizz as “blackberry, dragonfruit, and sparkle.” Those phrases are your map. Use them to name what you like, then ask a couple of ingredient questions at the counter. (Facts below are from the current menu; suggestions are clearly labeled.)

## What each menu description actually tells you

- Mango Chili Lime — mango, lime, and a sweet-heat finish. The description signals a fruit-forward base (mango and lime) with a noticeable sweet-plus-spicy ending. That “sweet-heat finish” implies something that warms or tingles the palate after the fruit notes.

- Passionfruit Mint — passionfruit, mint, and citrus. This pairs tropical tang (passionfruit) with a cooling herb (mint) and a bright citrus element. Expect the flavor profile to emphasize freshness and high notes rather than dessert sweetness.

- Blackberry Dragonfruit Fizz — blackberry, dragonfruit, and sparkle. “Sparkle” points to carbonation or a lively effervescence or simply a bright, crisp finish; paired with berry and exotic fruit notes, this one reads as light and fizzy rather than creamy.

(These descriptions are quoted directly from the menu for accuracy.)

## How to state your preference at the counter — short scripts that work

Use a single clear sentence that ties to those menu words. Pick whichever fits and say it just like this:

- If you want sweet with a kick: “I like mango-forward with a little heat — is Mango Chili Lime similar to that?”
- If you prefer cool and bright: “I usually pick mint and citrus — would Passionfruit Mint match that?”
- If you want something lighter and fizzy: “I’m after berry and a bubbly finish — is Blackberry Dragonfruit Fizz what you’d recommend?”

These lines do two things: they use the menu language the staff already uses, and they open the door for the kavatender to clarify ingredients. When you start this way the counter team can answer quickly and follow up with specifics.

## Questions to ask (what the menu won’t tell you)

Ask these before you order. They’re practical and short.

- Which botanical leaf is in this crafted kratom today — red, green, or white? The lounge serves brewed kratom tea and crafted kratom drinks, and leaf choice can vary.
- Is the “sweet-heat” from fresh chili, a chili syrup, or another spice? How strong is it typically?
- For citrus notes: is that from fresh lime/lemon juice or from a citrus-flavored syrup? Fresh juice will taste different than syrup.
- Does “sparkle” mean carbonation, a sparkling water base, or simply a bright flavor profile?
- Any added sweeteners or syrups I should know about?

These are simple ingredient questions. The menu and the New Here guidance both encourage asking about ingredients, so staff will expect these sorts of clarifications.

## Practical advice (clearly labeled suggestions)

- If you normally prefer iced fruit teas or bright cocktails, the Passionfruit Mint or Blackberry Dragonfruit Fizz are likely closer to your taste because both emphasize freshness and brightness.
- If you like tropical fruit with a spicy twist — think mango plus a warm finish — Mango Chili Lime matches that aim.
- Want to be cautious? Ask for a smaller size or request less sweetener if you’re unsure about how sugary or spicy a crafted drink will be.

These are suggestions to help you narrow a choice; they are not claims about effects.
