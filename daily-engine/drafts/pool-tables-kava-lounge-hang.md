## Questions to ask Tribal before you plan a pool-focused visit (these are things the site doesn’t state)

Before you move a whole group or schedule a night, message or call the lounge and confirm:

- Can pool tables be reserved or put on hold for a party? If so, how far in advance?
- Are there hourly table fees, a table minimum, or a time limit per table during busy hours?
- Is equipment provided (cues, chalk, triangle/rack)? If not, can you bring your own cues?
- Are there house rules (no food at the table, footwear policy, cue care, max players per table)?
- Are certain nights used for league play, tournament nights, or private events that would limit casual play?

Label these as questions to the team — they are not listed on the site and can change.

## Social and play-planning tips (practical, labeled)

Pre-game (planning)

- Pick a backup: If you can’t reserve a table, choose a backup plan for your group (board games, seated conversation, or alternating players) so no one’s left waiting.
- Coordinate arrival windows: Ask everyone to arrive within 15–30 minutes so you can claim a spot together rather than dribbling in across multiple hours.
- Check menu choices: The Menu page shows quick pours (kava shells and crafted drinks) and larger drinks. Plan whether people want something small between racks or a crafted drink that’s easier to sip while standing. (Source: Menu page.)

On-site (etiquette and logistics)

- Tell the staff you’re there to use a table — they can confirm availability and point you to the right seating section. The Visit page describes multiple seating areas; follow staff direction for where tables live. (Source: Visit page.)
- Respect the space: keep gear off couches, clear empty glasses, and follow any posted house rules for table care.
- Rotate fairly: if the lounge is busy, keep racks short (one or two games per person) so more patrons can enjoy the table.

If you want a quieter game night

- Aim for off-peak times: the Menu page lists happy-hour windows and the Visit page shows broad hours; a mid-afternoon or early-evening slot may be less crowded than late night. (Sources: Menu page; Visit page.)

Group formats that work well in a lounge setting

- Mini-tournament: bracket-style (single elimination) works in short games and gives everyone a clear schedule.
- Rotation play: each rack winner stays, or use a cut-stick method to keep rotation moving.

Bring or borrow?

- If you have a favorite cue, bring it. If you plan to rely on house cues, ask the staff about condition and availability in advance.
